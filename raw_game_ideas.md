Make a tower defense game. The game should feel polished, strategic, fast-paced, rewarding, and highly replayable. The player should feel like they are operating from a large defense headquarters where they prepare equipment, manage towers, unlock upgrades, collect rewards, and choose missions before entering actual battles. The main gameplay loop should be entering the main lobby, checking rewards and progression, managing the tower inventory, buying or unlocking new towers and cosmetics, choosing a mission, entering a smaller mission-preparation lobby, changing the equipped towers and map, starting the mission, building defenses, surviving waves, defeating bosses, earning rewards, returning to headquarters, improving the account, and repeating the process with harder missions and better equipment. When the player first opens the game, they should enter a large main headquarters lobby. This lobby should feel alive even though the game is completely single-player. The lobby should have animated screens, machinery, moving lights, tower displays, weapon testing areas, elevators, doors, mission screens, background activity, alarms, decorations, moving equipment, rotating tower models, environmental sounds, and changing details based on the player's progression. The lobby should feel like the player's personal defense command center rather than just a menu. The main spawn area should be in the center of the headquarters, with important areas visible from where the player starts. The player should be able to walk around the lobby, but every major feature should also be accessible through clear buttons on the main interface so the player never has to walk across the lobby just to use something important. The Inventory button should open the player's complete collection of towers. The inventory should display every tower the player owns and towers that are still locked. Each tower card should show the tower name, model, role, level, experience, current skin, important abilities, detection types, placement cost, general strengths, and upgrade progress. The player should be able to select a tower to inspect it in more detail, rotate its model, preview its different upgrade appearances, view its statistics, equip or unequip it, change its skin, view its special abilities, and see what is required to unlock additional upgrades or evolved versions. The player should have a limited number of equipped tower slots, forcing them to create a loadout before entering a battle. The equipped loadout should always be visible somewhere in the inventory so the player clearly understands which towers will be available during the next mission. The lobby should also contain a large Shop area. The Shop should allow the player to purchase new towers, skins, crates, cosmetics, emotes, profile decorations, consumables, and other permanent upgrades. The Shop should have different categories so it does not feel cluttered. There should be a Tower Shop where new towers can be purchased after meeting their level or progression requirements, a Daily Shop with rotating skins and items, a Crate Shop where cosmetic crates can be purchased and opened, a Consumables Shop where limited-use battle items can be purchased, and a Special Shop containing rare or late-game items. The player should be able to preview anything before purchasing it. A rotating tower display or hologram should show selected towers and skins directly inside the shop area. The lobby should include a Crate Opening area where the player can open cosmetic crates. Opening a crate should have a full animation with the crate appearing, lights flashing, possible rewards cycling by, sound effects building suspense, and then the final reward being revealed with its rarity. The player should immediately be able to equip the new skin after receiving it. The lobby should include a Missions area where the player selects what type of battle they want to enter. The Missions screen should show several categories such as Survival, Story, Hardcore, Challenge, Event, and Sandbox. Survival should contain the normal difficulty progression. Early difficulties should be designed for basic towers and shorter matches, while later difficulties should contain more waves, stronger enemies, dangerous abilities, and powerful bosses. Story missions should contain dialogue, cutscenes, special objectives, scripted events, unique bosses, and locations that explain the world and history of the game. Hardcore should contain extremely difficult missions designed for advanced towers and experienced players. Challenge missions should change the normal rules by adding modifiers such as faster enemies, limited towers, stronger bosses, reduced income, special weather, restricted tower categories, or unusual map conditions. Event missions should appear during special updates and introduce temporary maps, enemies, rewards, and tower unlocks. Sandbox should allow the player to freely experiment with towers, enemies, money, maps, and upgrades. The lobby should include a Quests menu. Daily and weekly quests should automatically track progress. Quests can ask the player to defeat enemies, deal damage, complete certain difficulties, use certain tower categories, defeat bosses, earn a specific amount of cash, place support towers, complete missions without losing base health, or use special abilities. Quests should reward coins, gems, crates, skins, tickets, experience, or special resources. The lobby should include an Index. The Index should act like a collection book containing information about enemies, maps, bosses, towers, achievements, and discovered secrets. Enemy entries should unlock after encountering or defeating them. Each enemy entry can show its model, health, speed, abilities, defenses, detection requirements, weaknesses, and where it appears. Map entries should show the map image, difficulty rating, completion status, fastest completion time, number of victories, best wave reached, available modes, and hidden objectives. Boss entries should show lore, abilities, battle statistics, and whether the boss has been defeated. The lobby should include Login Rewards that reward the player for returning on different days. Later days should provide better rewards. The lobby should include Playtime Rewards that unlock after the player has been active for certain amounts of time. These rewards should continue progressing both inside the lobby and during battles. The lobby should contain a Spin Wheel that uses Spin Tickets. The wheel can reward coins, gems, crates, consumables, skins, rare items, or special resources. The lobby should contain a Season or Battle Pass system where playing missions earns seasonal experience and unlocks rewards along a progression track. The pass can contain skins, crates, currency, consumables, visual effects, tower cosmetics, lobby decorations, and unique rewards. The lobby should contain a Codes menu where promotional codes can be entered for special rewards. It should clearly show whether the entered code is valid, expired, already used, or successful. The lobby should contain an Achievements area where the player can view completed and unfinished achievements. Achievements should encourage unusual challenges such as winning without economy towers, defeating a boss using only certain tower classes, completing a difficult map without taking base damage, reaching high player levels, discovering secrets, or mastering certain towers. The lobby should include a Trophy Room that physically changes as the player progresses. Boss trophies, completed difficulty medals, event trophies, rare tower statues, story artifacts, and achievement rewards should appear here. This makes progression visible inside the headquarters instead of only existing as numbers in menus. The lobby should also contain hidden rooms and secrets. Certain walls, vents, doors, terminals, or strange objects could lead to secret areas containing lore, collectible documents, cosmetic rewards, hidden achievements, strange enemy experiments, or hints about future missions. The headquarters should slowly change as the player progresses through the story. After major bosses are defeated, new rooms can open, damaged areas can be repaired, new technology can appear, captured enemy equipment can be displayed, and new characters or machines can become available. When the player chooses a mission from the main lobby, do not immediately send them into the battle. Instead, transition the player into a smaller mission preparation lobby. This smaller lobby should feel like a briefing room or deployment chamber connected to the selected mission. The environment should visually match the mission. A desert map might have sand blowing near a loading door, a winter mission might show frost and snow outside, a ruined city mission might show smoke and emergency lights, and a high-tech mission might have holograms and machinery. At the bottom of the screen, clearly display all towers currently equipped in the player's loadout. Each equipped tower should have its icon, name, current skin, and possibly its role. The player should be able to click any equipped slot to replace that tower before starting the mission. Opening an equipped slot should bring up the player's tower inventory so they can quickly choose a different tower without returning to the main lobby. The player should also be able to rearrange the order of equipped towers because this order should match their hotbar during the mission. The preparation lobby should contain a large map display. The selected map should appear as a detailed preview with an image or 3D holographic version of the battlefield. The player should be able to change maps if the selected mode allows multiple maps. The map selection screen should show important information such as map length, number of lanes, amount of placement space, cliff locations, special hazards, recommended difficulty, previous best completion time, best wave reached, and possible unique mechanics. The player should also be able to change the selected difficulty or mode from this smaller lobby without returning to headquarters. In the smaller app the player should be able to change the map and their towers they have equipped. The preparation room should show a mission briefing describing the type of enemies expected. Instead of revealing every enemy, it can give useful information such as Heavy Armor Detected, Flying Enemies Expected, Hidden Units Expected, Large Enemy Groups Expected, Boss-Class Threat Detected, or Environmental Hazard Present. This allows the player to adjust their tower loadout before starting. The preparation lobby should include a Start button. When the player presses Start, lock the selected map, difficulty, loadout, and consumables, play a deployment animation, and transition into the actual battlefield. The mission should begin with a short cinematic camera movement showing the enemy spawn area, the path, important terrain, and the player's base before returning control to the player. During the battle, the player should freely move around the battlefield and place towers near the enemy route. Enemies should automatically travel from their entrance toward the player's base. If they reach the base, they damage its health. If base health reaches zero, the player loses the mission. The player's equipped towers should appear at the bottom of the screen in the same order selected in the preparation lobby. Each tower icon should show its placement cost. Selecting a tower should create a transparent placement preview that follows the player's cursor. A range circle should show how far the tower can attack. Valid placement areas should be clearly different from invalid areas. Ground towers should require valid ground, cliff towers should require elevated positions, and special towers may have unique placement rules. Once placed, towers automatically attack enemies inside their range. Clicking a placed tower should open its information panel. The panel should show the tower name, level, damage, attack speed, range, DPS when appropriate, detection types, targeting mode, abilities, upgrade cost, sell value, and special effects. The player should be able to upgrade, sell, activate abilities, and change targeting from this menu. Every tower should have multiple upgrade levels. Upgrades should improve statistics but should also visually transform the tower. Weapons can become larger, armor can become more advanced, effects can become brighter, new equipment can appear, or the tower can completely change its appearance at high levels. Towers should have very different gameplay roles. Starter towers should be cheap and reliable. Damage towers should focus on direct damage. Splash towers should damage groups. Support towers should increase attack speed, damage, range, or reduce costs. Economy towers should generate additional money. Control towers should slow, freeze, stun, knock back, poison, weaken, or otherwise disrupt enemies. Spawner towers should create friendly soldiers, vehicles, creatures, drones, machines, or other units that move along the enemy path. Flying towers should patrol or circle above the battlefield. Melee towers should attack enemies at very close range. Cliff towers should use elevated positions for long-range attacks. Some towers should specialize against bosses. When a boss does appear it should show an icon on what the boss's HP is. Others should specialize against large enemy groups. Some should provide detection for Hidden, Flying, armored, or unusual enemy types. The player should never be able to solve every situation using only one tower. Creating a balanced loadout should be an important part of the strategy. Battles should be divided into waves. Early waves should start slowly and contain simple enemies so the player can establish a defense. Each new wave should gradually increase the pressure. New enemy types should appear over time. Fast enemies should move quickly but have low health. Heavy enemies should move slowly but have large amounts of health. Hidden enemies should require Hidden detection. Flying enemies should require towers capable of attacking air targets. Armored enemies should resist certain attacks. Shielded enemies should have extra protection. Regenerating enemies should slowly restore health. Summoners should create smaller enemies. Support enemies should buff nearby enemies. Stunning enemies should temporarily disable towers. Teleporting enemies could jump forward along the path. Splitting enemies could create smaller enemies after being defeated. Later waves should combine several enemy types at once to create more complex threats. The player should earn battle cash by defeating enemies, damaging enemies where appropriate, completing waves, and using economy towers. Battle cash should only exist inside the current mission and should be used to place towers, upgrade towers, and activate certain abilities. Permanent currencies such as coins and gems should only be earned after or between missions. The player should be able to start the next wave early using a Skip Wave button. Skipping should provide faster gameplay but reduce preparation time. Boss waves should feel dramatically different from normal waves. Bosses should have large models, unique music, special animations, a large health bar, multiple abilities, and possibly several phases. Bosses could stun towers, summon enemies, destroy temporary defenses, change speed, create shields, alter the battlefield, disable areas, or force the player to change strategy during the fight. Some bosses should physically change appearance as their health decreases. Losing a mission should show the wave reached, enemies defeated, damage dealt, towers placed, money earned, strongest tower, and rewards earned from progress. Winning should display a Triumph screen containing the completed mode, map, time, rewards, account experience, tower experience, quests completed, achievements progressed, and any newly unlocked content. The player should then choose whether to replay the mission, return to the mission preparation lobby, or return to headquarters. Add a completely new signature mechanic called the Tactical Command System to make this game stand out from normal tower defense games. During a mission, the player should slowly build Command Energy by defeating enemies, completing waves, avoiding base damage, and using towers effectively. Command Energy fills a meter separate from money. When enough energy is collected, the player can activate powerful battlefield commands from a Command Menu. These are not normal tower abilities. They represent the player directly controlling the entire defense operation. Commands could include Recon Scan, which temporarily reveals all hidden enemies and shows upcoming enemies before they spawn, Emergency Supply Drop, which gives a temporary burst of battle cash or reduces upgrade costs, Overclock Network, which temporarily increases the attack speed of every tower, Tactical Freeze, which briefly slows every enemy on the map, Emergency Relocation, which allows one placed tower to be moved to a different valid location without selling it, Target Priority Order, which causes all towers to focus a selected boss or dangerous enemy for several seconds, Reinforcement Drop, which temporarily sends friendly soldiers or vehicles onto the path, Air Support, which calls in aircraft to attack a selected section of the battlefield, and Last Stand, which activates only when the base reaches critically low health and temporarily strengthens defenses. The player should not be able to spam these commands. Command Energy should build slowly, forcing the player to decide whether to use a smaller command now or save energy for a more powerful one later. Add another unique system called Dynamic Threats. Some maps should change during the mission instead of remaining identical from Wave 1 to the final wave. A bridge could collapse and create a new route, a storm could reduce tower range, power could fail and darken the map, enemy artillery could temporarily block a placement area, an earthquake could open a second lane, frozen water could become walkable, a destroyed wall could create a shortcut for enemies, or the final boss could physically alter the path. Before these changes occur, the Command Center should provide warnings so the player has time to react. This makes maps feel like active battlefields rather than static paths. Add a Preparation Intelligence system to connect the main lobby, mission lobby, and battle. Before a mission, the player can spend Intelligence Points earned from previous victories to uncover additional information about the mission. The first level might reveal general enemy categories. The second could reveal approximately when flying or hidden enemies appear. Higher intelligence could reveal boss abilities, environmental changes, or possible secret objectives. The player must decide whether to spend Intelligence Points to learn more or save them for later missions. Add secret mission objectives that are not required to win but reward skilled players. Examples include taking zero base damage, defeating a boss before a certain time, using fewer than a certain number of towers, discovering a hidden object on the map, protecting a secondary structure, preventing an enemy from reaching a checkpoint, completing the mission without selling towers, or activating a hidden event during a certain wave. Completing secret objectives can unlock special cosmetics, tower upgrades, lore, alternate map versions, or rare equipment. Add a Tower Mastery system. Every tower should gain its own experience when used. Reaching mastery milestones should unlock cosmetic upgrades, alternate visual effects, special profile badges, tower lore, statistics, and eventually small side-grade options. Side-grades should not simply make towers stronger. Instead they should allow different playstyles. For example, a tower could choose between longer range or faster attacks, stronger boss damage or stronger crowd control, better support buffs or stronger personal damage. This lets experienced players customize towers without making earlier progression useless. Add a Headquarters Upgrade system. The player's main lobby itself should improve as the account progresses. The starting headquarters can look basic and partially unfinished. Completing major missions should unlock new rooms, technology, displays, decorations, research stations, training areas, command screens, and trophy displays. The headquarters should visually show how far the player has progressed through the game. Add a Research Lab inside the lobby. Research should use resources earned from missions to unlock permanent utility features instead of direct damage upgrades. Research examples include better mission intelligence, additional loadout presets, more detailed enemy information, improved reward previews, expanded Sandbox controls, extra cosmetic options, more Command System choices, or new challenge modifiers. Add loadout presets so the player can save several tower combinations for different strategies such as economy, boss damage, crowd control, flying defense, or challenge missions. These presets should be accessible from both the main Inventory and the smaller preparation lobby. Add a Mission History terminal in the lobby showing previous missions, completion times, rewards, damage statistics, tower usage, boss kill times, base health remaining, and personal records. The player should be able to select an old mission and inspect exactly what towers were used. Add a Bestiary Research feature where repeatedly defeating an enemy increases research percentage on that enemy. At low research, only basic information is available. At higher research levels, the player discovers exact statistics, resistances, abilities, weaknesses, and lore. Fully researching rare enemies could unlock special rewards. Add a changing lobby atmosphere based on the player's current selected mission or story progress. If the player selects a dangerous mission, warning lights might activate inside the headquarters. If an ice event is happening, frost could appear around certain doors. After defeating an important boss, its damaged equipment or trophy could appear inside the lobby. If the story reaches a crisis point, the headquarters can visually look damaged until the next chapter is completed. The game should feel like one connected world instead of separate menus. The most important feeling should be watching a small defense grow into a massive battlefield. A mission might begin with one cheap tower beside an empty road. By the final waves, the battlefield should contain upgraded soldiers, heavy weapons, support towers, aircraft, summoned units, explosions, electricity, fire, ice, missiles, energy weapons, command abilities, environmental changes, and an enormous boss moving toward the base. The player should always feel like their decisions created that defensive machine. The headquarters provides preparation and progression, the smaller mission lobby provides final strategy and loadout control, the battle provides action and decision-making, the Tactical Command System gives the player direct battlefield influence, Dynamic Threats make maps unpredictable, Tower Mastery creates long-term attachment to individual towers, and the evolving headquarters makes progression physically visible. The complete gameplay loop should feel like Headquarters, collect rewards, check quests, visit shops, manage inventory, choose a mission, enter the mission preparation room, inspect the map and threats, change equipped towers and consumables, press Start, deploy to the battlefield, place defenses, earn cash, upgrade towers, respond to changing threats, use Tactical Commands, survive increasingly difficult waves, defeat the boss, complete optional objectives, earn rewards and mastery experience, return to headquarters, unlock new content, improve the command center, and prepare for the next mission. Every part of the game should make the player want to improve their strategy, unlock something new, try another tower combination, beat a harder difficulty, discover another secret, or achieve a better result on a mission they have already completed.
